This was my first time at FOSSGIS and also my first time attending a conference entirely in German 🇩🇪. Having lived in Germany for over six years now, it was great to finally experience a conference in the native tongue of my chosen country of residence 🥲. To keep the post organized, I'll give my take on a few of my favorite talks from each day. I'll also mention the talks I didn't see that day that I want to watch later online. I wasn't able to attend the first day, so this post begins on Thursday (March 26th).

Overall, the conference was an amazing experience. I felt like each talk I saw had something unique to offer and because this was my first GIS themed talk, everything felt new and fresh.

## Thursday 2026-03-26

### Geocoding - maßgeschneidert

This was a demon hosted by one of the maintainers of the Nominatim API. In the talk, we were walked through the process of create a small and focused geocoding server. The example showed how we might go about adding a feature to a tourism website about Göttingen.

I though this was very informative and might be a really useful method for me to replicate for my own online maps. Rather than relying on a paid geocoding service, I could use something like this instead.

### Open-Source-Tools und Citizen Science für urbane Mobilitätsdaten

I was extremely impressed by all the moving pieces in this talk! By far, the coolest piece of technology shown were the data collection devices used by the bikes that feed their analysis. This reminded me of project I attempted several years back to create my own GPS/temperature sensing devices with Arduino.

### Aufbau und Aktualisierung einer OSM-basierten Karten mit osm2pgsql

This talk was the most interesting to me because I have been recently working on tools that kind of do the same thing (organized import and updating of OpenStreetMap (OSM) data into PostgreSQL). Some of the methods used were very similar to what I showed in [my own PyCon DE 22 talk](/posts/2022-04-02-processing-osm-data-with-postgresql-and-python), but the presenter went even further by demoing a custom QGIS plugin they wrote in order reconcile differences in the data that has changed. Very impressive!

I'll be keeping an eye on his work and also keeping a close eye on osm2pgsql developments, especially the "themepark" beta feature.

### OpenStreetMap-Daten als Grundlage für Routing – wie gut funktioniert das wirklich?

A project directly from HeiGT. For those that don't know, they are a research institution based out of the University of Heidelberg and have delivered amazing projects like OpenRoutingService in the past. This talk focused on why it is that routes calculated in African countries and India differ so drastically from each other.

To help over explain this, they introduce a tool called ohsome that attempts to explain the quality of data used in calculating a route by look at metadata associated with it (e.g. how long ago was the speed on a certain street updated?). I'm definitely going back later to use this tool fro myself and explore its capabilities more thoroughly.

## Saturday 2026-03-28

This day was a bit different from the two days of the official conference I was able to attend. The format was an unconference themed around OSM, which meant that everyone had a chance to propose a topic at the beginning of the day and place on the schedule as they wished.

### Verbesserungen für openstreetmap.org

Eike Send proposed a set a improvements that we he wanted to make to openstreetmap.org by switching to a system that relies on vector tiles rather than the raster tiles currently being used. Having just learned more about vector tiles the day before, I was all ears and found [the demo](https://maps.eike.se/) that he share very interesting!

### Mit OSM die Verkehrswende begleiten und beschleunigen

Last but certainly not least is the session that was hosted again by employees of `<insert company name>`. This presentation was a year in review of all the things that had been happening with this community group focused on bring about a less car-centric future of urban transportation in Germany.
